What's really going on

What training does to your muscles

Training makes you stronger by breaking muscle down - and recovery is where the adaptation actually happens. The problem is that when you train hard and often, tension accumulates faster than the body clears it. Muscles stay short and tight, fascia stiffens, small trigger points form, and your range of motion quietly shrinks without you noticing.

And you work around it before you admit it. You drop the last set. You skip the sprint day. You warm up twice as long and still don't trust the movement. The training doesn't stop - it just quietly gets smaller.

You feel it as sessions that don't move the way they should, tightness that won't stretch out, and soreness that lingers longer than it used to. Left unmanaged, that build-up is exactly how nagging tightness turns into the strain or overuse injury that costs you weeks off. Ivan has a sports background himself, so he understands first-hand how training, competition and repetitive movement load the body - and what it takes to keep it moving.

Why a relaxation massage doesn't fix it

A relaxation massage is pleasant, but it's passive - it isn't built to move a trained, loaded muscle. Sports recovery work is targeted: it addresses the specific muscles you're overloading, drives circulation through the tissue, releases the trigger points limiting your range, and restores the glide between muscle layers so you move freely again. That's a completely different job from a spa hour, and it's what actually shortens recovery time between sessions.

How the session is structured

Ivan starts with what you train, what's tight, and what's coming up - a race, a heavy block, or simply staying ahead of the wear and tear. Then he works the muscles doing the most for your sport, combining deeper techniques to release built-up tension, trigger point therapy on the spots limiting your movement, and stretching and myofascial work to give range back. Timing shapes the session: heavier, deeper work when you have recovery days ahead, lighter flushing work when an event is close. Everything is matched to your training load, not a fixed template.

What to expect after

Most people leave with more range and lighter legs or arms. If the work was deep, expect a day of mild worked-muscle soreness - which is why deeper sessions are best placed away from key workouts or events. Hydrate, keep moving easily, and you'll usually feel the benefit in your next session. Ivan will often suggest how to time your next visit around your training so you get the most out of it.

How many sessions you'll need

It depends on your load. If you train several times a week, a session every one to two weeks keeps tension from accumulating and protects your range of motion. Around a race or a heavy block you might come in more often; if you're recovering from a specific overload, a short series close together resets it faster. The 4-pack ($300) and 8-pack ($590) suit athletes who'd rather stay ahead of problems than chase them. Ivan builds the cadence around your calendar.
