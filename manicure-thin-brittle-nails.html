What's really going on

Why nails go thin and brittle

Nails don't peel and break for no reason. Most of the time they've been thinned out: by rough filing, by aggressive removal, by thick layers of acrylic that got scraped off instead of dissolved. Every one of those passes takes a micro-layer of the nail itself. So over time the plate gets thinner, bends, and cracks right at the edge.

And now you're not getting a set for the look — you're getting one because without a coating the nail just breaks. That isn't care. It's a loop.

Why the average salon makes it worse

In a rush, they file the nail "to a squeak," strip the gel dry, and hurry. You come in for beauty — and leave with an even thinner nail. And the loop repeats: a thin nail breaks, it gets filed again, and it gets thinner still.

The problem isn't that your nails are "just like that." The problem is that they've been steadily ground down.

How we work with thin nails

We don't file the nail. We remove the coating gently, working the e-file only on the dead tissue, never on the living plate. Where the nail is soft and bends, we reinforce it with a thin layer of gel — a light splint that holds the shape, keeps it from cracking, and lets it grow out underneath. No "beauty at any cost."

What to expect after

The first visit stops the damage. From there, refill to refill, a stronger nail of your own grows in. Most people forget what "breaks at the edge" feels like after 2–3 months of consistent care.

The question isn't whether your nails will reach the length you want. It's how much longer you'll keep filing them down.
