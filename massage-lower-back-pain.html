What's really going on

Why your lower back keeps hurting

Lower back pain is the tension pattern most people learn to live with. It starts as a dull ache after sitting, becomes a morning stiffness that takes an hour to loosen, and eventually turns into a sharp catch when you bend, twist or lift. In Aventura we see it in drivers, desk workers, new parents, and anyone whose job or training loads the same muscles day after day.

And you adapt without noticing. You get out of the car differently. You let someone else lift the case. You've picked a side of the bed based on which way you can roll out of it. That's the real cost - not the pain itself, but the list of small things you've quietly rearranged around it.

Here's the part most people miss: the pain usually isn't coming from where you feel it. The lower back is where the body reports the problem - but the cause is often tight hip flexors, a locked quadratus lumborum, weak deep stabilisers, or fascia around the lumbar spine that has stopped gliding the way it should. Sit for hours and your glutes switch off, so the lower back ends up doing work it was never designed to do. That's why it keeps coming back: you rest, it calms down, you return to the same movement pattern, and the same muscles overload all over again.

Why a relaxation massage doesn't fix it

A spa-style relaxation massage feels good for a day. It warms the surface, calms the nervous system and sends you home relaxed - but it never reaches the muscles actually driving the pain. Light, general strokes don't release a trigger point buried in the quadratus lumborum, and they don't restore movement to fascia that has become stiff and stuck. So the relief fades by the next morning and you're back where you started. Therapeutic work is a different job: it locates the specific structures holding the tension and works them directly.

How the session is structured

Every session with Ivan starts with a short assessment - where the pain sits, when it flares, which movements trigger it, and what you want to be able to do again without thinking about your back. From there he works the whole chain, not just the sore spot: hips, glutes, the lumbar muscles and the surrounding fascia. He uses deep tissue massage to reach the deeper layers, trigger point therapy to release the knots referring pain into your back, and myofascial release to restore glide to the tissue. Pressure is matched to what your body tolerates that day - deep enough to change something, never so much that you brace against it. Ivan has a sports background, so he reads how you move and adjusts in real time rather than running a fixed routine.

What to expect after

Most people stand up with noticeably more room - bending, turning and straightening feel easier before you even leave the room. It's normal to feel a mild, worked-muscle soreness for a day, similar to the day after a good workout; drink water, keep moving gently, and it settles. A lot of clients describe the night after as the deepest sleep they've had in months. Ivan will usually give you one or two simple things to do between visits so the change holds instead of slipping back.
