What's really going on

What's left after a bad set

After a rough removal of acrylic or gel, the nail often comes out thinned, with whitish patches, rough and soft. Sometimes with hangnails and an irritated cuticle. This isn't "ruined forever" - it's the top layers of the plate, taken off along with the coating.

The bad news: that layer isn't coming back. The good news: the nail grows in again from the root, and with the right care what grows will be healthy.

Why "just re-cover it" doesn't fix it

The easiest move is to mask it: lay a new thick layer on top and you can't see it. But underneath, the nail isn't recovering - it thins further at the next removal. Masking isn't recovery. So a couple of sets later it's only worse, and you're still hiding your hands.

How we rebuild it

First we look honestly at what's really going on with the nail. We remove the old coating gently, with no over-filing of the plate. The e-file touches only the dead tissue around it. Where the nail is soft and bends, we add a thin reinforcement so it doesn't break while the healthy part grows. And then we simply carry you refill to refill until the "ruined" nail is replaced by your own.

What to expect after

A nail renews from the root in about 3-4 months. The whole time it's protected, doesn't break, and looks tidy - no walking around "ashamed" for half a year. The white patches and rough texture grow out as the nail does.

You've already lost your nails in someone else's chair. The only question left is whose chair they recover in.
