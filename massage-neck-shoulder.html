What's really going on

Why desk work locks up your neck and shoulders

Neck and shoulder pain from desk work builds so gradually you don't notice until it's constant. Hours at a screen pull the head forward, round the shoulders and lock the upper traps, the levator scapulae and the small muscles at the base of the skull into a permanent contraction. By mid-afternoon your neck feels stiff; by evening your shoulders sit up near your ears and won't drop.

By then it's running your day quietly. You roll your shoulders in meetings. You tilt the laptop instead of turning your head. You've started reversing out of the driveway using the mirrors, because looking over your shoulder isn't worth it.

There's a mechanical reason it gets worse the longer you sit. For every inch your head drifts forward of your shoulders, the muscles holding it up work dramatically harder — so a full day at a desk is really hours of low-grade overload. Over months that tension hardens into trigger points, restricted fascia and a forward-head posture that holds even after you stand up. It's one of the most common reasons people in Aventura come to see Ivan.

Why a relaxation massage doesn't fix it

A general relaxation massage glides over the shoulders and feels pleasant — but desk-work tension lives in specific, deep muscles: the levator scapulae where it grips the shoulder blade, the knots buried in the upper trapezius, the tiny suboccipital muscles under the skull that refer pain up into headaches. Light, sweeping strokes never reach them. You leave relaxed, and by the next morning back at your desk the tightness has already returned, because nothing underneath it actually changed.

How the session is structured

Ivan starts by looking at how you actually hold yourself and where the pain sits — the base of the skull, the tops of the shoulders, the area between the shoulder blades. Then he works the whole upper chain: releasing the upper traps and levator scapulae, freeing the muscles between and beneath the shoulder blades, using trigger point therapy on the knots that fire pain into your head, and myofascial release to give the tissue its movement back. He'll often work the neck and the muscles under the skull directly, because that's where desk-related tension headaches usually begin. Pressure is always matched to you, and he adjusts as he goes.

What to expect after

Most people can turn their head further and drop their shoulders for the first time in weeks before they leave. Tension headaches often ease the same day. How many sessions you'll need

If the tension is recent, a single session can resolve most of it. If you've been building it for months or years at a desk, expect to need a few sessions relatively close together to fully unwind it, then a maintenance visit every three to four weeks to keep it from creeping back — because you'll keep returning to the same desk.
